Not every water heater job is a straight replacement. Some Huntington, WV homeowners are finishing a basement or building an addition and need a second heater. Other jobs involve moving a unit out of a cramped closet, stepping up to a bigger tank for a growing family, or switching fuels entirely. All of them mean new gas piping, vent routes, circuits, or water lines, planned out before anyone cuts a pipe. We take on installations of every size, from a small point-of-use heater under a sink to a whole-house tankless system.

Sizing and placement shape most of the outcome. For a tank, we compare first-hour ratings against your household's busiest hour; for a tankless unit, we size by flow rate and winter temperature rise. Then comes placement. A gas heater needs a vent route and combustion air, a heat pump heater needs open space and a condensate drain, and a whole-house electric tankless unit usually needs a big dedicated circuit and matching panel capacity. Any heater placed above a finished ceiling needs a drain pan, and a gas unit in a garage may need to sit on a raised platform where code requires it. You get the trade-offs in plain terms, with no pressure.

On installation day, nothing is left to guesswork. New gas piping is run and pressure-tested, a sediment trap goes on, and the vent is routed exactly to spec. For electric and heat pump units, the circuit is matched to the load. Water lines go in with accessible shutoffs and proper transitions between dissimilar metals, and the relief valve is piped full size to a safe discharge point. Where code requires an expansion tank, drain pan, or seismic straps, we add them, and we pull the permit when your jurisdiction needs one. Last, the heater is filled, gas models are checked for draft and combustion, and the temperature is set safely. Planning a new installation? Call (304) 247-3882 for an assessment in Huntington.

When to Call for Water Heater Installation

Catching a symptom early is often the difference between a quick part swap and a tank that gives out on the garage floor. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Plans to change fuel types or go all-electric
  • Morning showers that run cold for the last person
  • A large soaking tub that a standard tank cannot fill
  • Remodel plans that add fixtures or a second bathroom
  • A tank in a tight spot that is hard to service or replace
  • Taps at the far end of the house that take minutes to warm up
  • A property where the water heater has to go in from scratch
  • Missing code items like an expansion tank, drain pan, or proper venting

Why This Job Calls for a Pro

Installing a water heater from scratch involves gas, venting, electrical, and plumbing work all at once. Gas lines need sizing for the whole system's load, plus a leak test. A vent outside the manufacturer's limits on length, diameter, slope, or clearance can put carbon monoxide into living space. Electric, tankless, and heat pump heaters need circuits that match their load, sometimes more than an older panel can supply. On the plumbing side, the relief valve needs the right rating and a full-size discharge line, and depending on location, code may require an expansion tank, a drain pan, seismic straps, or a permit and inspection.

Professional installation gets every piece right the first time. Our experienced technicians size the heater, calculate the gas line, lay out the vent route, confirm electrical capacity, and add the code items your location calls for. We test for leaks, check draft and carbon monoxide on gas units, and leave the heater at a safe temperature with the paperwork in order. It is the gap between a heater that just works for years and one that fails inspection or backdrafts when the weather turns. Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

Routine maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Descale tankless units once a year, or more often with hard water
  • Keep the temperature around 120 degrees; hotter settings raise the risk of scalding
  • Do not box the heater in; leave room for service and airflow
  • Moisture in the drain pan or at the relief line is an early warning worth a call
  • Have the anode rod inspected every few years to protect the tank
  • Start a flushing routine early, before sediment has a chance to harden
  • Keep flammable liquids and clutter away from gas heaters
