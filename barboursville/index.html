Keeping Your Barboursville Water Heater Running Longer

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, test the relief valve once a year to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which limits scalding risk and slows mineral buildup. Tankless owners should plan on descaling once a year in hard-water areas.

How old is your water heater? The rating plate on the tank usually encodes the month and year it was built, and that date says a lot about what comes next. Most tank models make it to 8 to 12 years, heat pump units 10 to 15, and well-maintained tankless units about 20. If your heater is beyond that point and is also showing discolored water, popping sounds, or moisture at the base, it's a strong candidate for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than a rushed emergency install once the tank fails.

Left alone, a small problem tends to grow. Consider a spent anode rod: swapping it out is a quick, affordable repair, but if nobody replaces it, the water starts eating the tank steel, and suddenly a minor fix has become a full replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to save our number in their phone and call at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and prevents a flooded utility room.

Hot Water Headaches Barboursville Homeowners Face

Every type of water heater has its own weak points, and we work on all of them around Barboursville. Gas tank heaters tend to have trouble with thermocouples and flame sensors, sooty burners, and venting that doesn't draft. Electric units burn out elements and thermostats, particularly when sediment piles up around the lower element. Tankless heaters show error codes for scale buildup, ignition failures, and flow sensor problems, and many of those trace back to skipped descaling. Heat pump water heaters depend on a clean filter and a free-flowing condensate line, and they don't do well in small, closed-in spaces. We service all four types regularly and carry the parts to fix most problems on the first visit.
