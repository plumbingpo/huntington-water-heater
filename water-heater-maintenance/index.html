Here is what shortens most water heaters' lives: corrosion from the inside and mineral buildup on the bottom, both of which are largely preventable. As water heats, dissolved minerals drop out of solution and settle into a layer of sediment on the tank floor. On a gas unit, that layer sits between the flame and the water, so the steel runs hotter and the tank starts to rumble and pop. On an electric unit, it buries the lower element until the element burns out. Meanwhile the anode rod, a metal rod designed to corrode so the tank does not, dissolves a little every month. Once it is gone, the tank itself becomes the target. In Huntington, WV, we see this pattern every week, and most of it was preventable.

A Huntington Water Heater visit is designed to stop both of those processes early. We drain and flush the tank until the water runs clear, remove and check the anode rod and recommend a replacement when it is mostly consumed, and test the temperature and pressure relief valve to confirm it opens and reseats. On gas units we inspect the burner, pilot or igniter, thermocouple or flame sensor, flue baffle, and venting for proper draft. On electric units we check element resistance and thermostat operation and look at the wiring for heat damage. We check the expansion tank's air charge against your water pressure, confirm the thermostat setting is near 120 degrees to limit scalding, and look for early leaks at fittings and valves.

Tankless owners need maintenance too, just a different kind. Scale builds up inside the heat exchanger, especially in hard water, and eventually triggers error codes, reduced flow, or temperature swings. We descale the heat exchanger with a pump and descaling solution through the service valves, clean the inlet filter, check the venting and condensate line on condensing models, and review the unit's error history. When to Call for Water Heater Maintenance

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you money and keep a slow drip from turning into a flooded utility room. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Scale warnings or intermittent shutdowns on a tankless system
  • Moisture, drips, or rust around the base or fittings
  • Hot water running out sooner than it used to
  • A rotten-egg smell from the hot water
  • Noises from the water heater that were not there before
  • Hot water that looks cloudy or rust-colored
  • More than a year since anyone flushed the tank
  • Rising utility bills that point to lost efficiency

What a Trained Technician Brings to the Job

Some upkeep is fair game for homeowners: watching for leaks, keeping the area around the heater clear, and noting new noises. Beyond that, the work gets riskier. Draining a tank means handling scalding water and a drain valve that may not close again if it is old and full of sediment. Pulling an anode rod often takes serious torque on a fitting that has not moved in years. Testing a T&P valve on an older heater can leave it dripping afterward. On gas units, the burner, thermocouple, and venting involve combustion safety, and a vent that is not drafting can put carbon monoxide into the house.

Professional maintenance also brings the right parts on the truck, including drain valves, anode rods and flexible anodes, relief valves, and meters for electrical testing. Tankless descaling gear comes along for those units. Experience counts as much as equipment: an experienced eye can spot a corroding nipple, a sooty burner, or an expansion tank that has lost its charge long before any of them cause trouble. Tips to Keep Your Water Heater Healthy

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Here's where to begin:

  • Have the anode rod checked every few years and replaced when it is mostly gone
  • Ask about water treatment if flushing turns up heavy mineral buildup
  • Avoid turning the tank up past 120 degrees unless a mixing valve is installed
  • Plan yearly descaling for a tankless heater, sooner if scale warnings appear
  • Keep at least a couple of feet clear around the heater for air and service access
  • Make an annual flush part of your home maintenance routine
  • Check the drain pan and fittings every few months for drips or rust
